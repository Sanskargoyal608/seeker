# Clinical Conditions Research: Part 1 - Mood & Anxiety Disorders

## 1. Major Depressive Disorder (MDD)
- **Screening Tool**: Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9)
  - **Questions/Domains**: 9 items assessing DSM-5 criteria over the last 2 weeks: (1) Little interest/pleasure, (2) Feeling down/depressed/hopeless, (3) Sleep disturbances, (4) Fatigue/low energy, (5) Appetite changes, (6) Feeling bad about self/failure, (7) Trouble concentrating, (8) Psychomotor agitation/retardation, (9) Suicidal ideation.
- **DSM-5 Criteria Summary**: Five (or more) of 9 symptoms present during the same 2-week period; at least one of the symptoms is either (1) depressed mood or (2) loss of interest or pleasure. Must cause clinically significant distress.
- **Validation Papers**: 
  - Kroenke K, Spitzer RL, Williams JB. "The PHQ-9: validity of a brief depression severity measure." *Journal of General Internal Medicine*, 2001;16(9):606-613. DOI: 10.1046/j.1525-1497.2001.016009606.x
- **User Language/Keywords**: "can't get out of bed", "feel empty", "what's the point", "exhausted all the time", "lost my spark", "dark cloud".
- **Severity Scoring Interpretation**: 0-4 (None/Minimal), 5-9 (Mild), 10-14 (Moderate), 15-19 (Moderately Severe), 20-27 (Severe).

## 2. Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD)
- **Screening Tool**: Generalized Anxiety Disorder 7-item scale (GAD-7)
  - **Questions/Domains**: 7 items over last 2 weeks: (1) Feeling nervous, anxious or on edge, (2) Not being able to stop or control worrying, (3) Worrying too much about different things, (4) Trouble relaxing, (5) Being so restless that it is hard to sit still, (6) Becoming easily annoyed or irritable, (7) Feeling afraid as if something awful might happen.
- **DSM-5 Criteria Summary**: Excessive anxiety and worry occurring more days than not for at least 6 months, about a number of events or activities. Difficult to control the worry. Associated with ≥3 of 6 symptoms (restlessness, fatigue, poor concentration, irritability, muscle tension, sleep disturbance).
- **Validation Papers**: 
  - Spitzer RL, Kroenke K, Williams JB, Löwe B. "A brief measure for assessing generalized anxiety disorder: the GAD-7." *Archives of Internal Medicine*, 2006;166(10):1092-1097. DOI: 10.1001/archinte.166.10.1092
- **User Language/Keywords**: "always worried", "mind won't shut off", "can't relax", "muscle knots", "on edge", "overthinking".
- **Severity Scoring Interpretation**: 0-4 (Minimal), 5-9 (Mild), 10-14 (Moderate), 15-21 (Severe).

## 3. Social Anxiety Disorder (Social Phobia)
- **Screening Tool**: Social Phobia Inventory (SPIN)
  - **Questions/Domains**: 17 items assessing fear, avoidance, and physiological arousal in social situations (e.g., fear of authority figures, avoiding speaking to strangers, blushing/sweating).
- **DSM-5 Criteria Summary**: Marked fear or anxiety about one or more social situations in which the individual is exposed to possible scrutiny by others. Fear of negative evaluation. The situations almost always provoke anxiety and are avoided. Persists for ≥6 months.
- **Validation Papers**: 
  - Connor KM, et al. "Psychometric properties of the Social Phobia Inventory (SPIN)." *British Journal of Psychiatry*, 2000;176(4):379-386. DOI: 10.1192/bjp.176.4.379
- **User Language/Keywords**: "hate crowds", "afraid of looking stupid", "people are judging me", "panic when speaking", "awkward".
- **Severity Scoring Interpretation**: 0-20 (None), 21-30 (Mild), 31-40 (Moderate), 41-50 (Severe), 51-68 (Very Severe).

## 4. Panic Disorder
- **Screening Tool**: Panic Disorder Severity Scale (PDSS)
  - **Questions/Domains**: 7 items assessing frequency of panic attacks, distress during attacks, anticipatory anxiety, agoraphobic avoidance, interoceptive avoidance, impairment in work functioning, and impairment in social functioning.
- **DSM-5 Criteria Summary**: Recurrent unexpected panic attacks. At least one attack has been followed by 1 month (or more) of persistent concern about additional attacks or their consequences, or a significant maladaptive change in behavior related to the attacks.
- **Validation Papers**: 
  - Shear MK, et al. "Multicenter collaborative panic disorder severity scale." *American Journal of Psychiatry*, 1997;154(11):1571-1575. DOI: 10.1176/ajp.154.11.1571
- **User Language/Keywords**: "heart attack feeling", "sudden terror", "can't breathe", "dizziness out of nowhere", "losing control".
- **Severity Scoring Interpretation**: 0-1 (Normal), 2-5 (Borderline), 6-9 (Slightly Ill), 10-13 (Moderately Ill), 14-28 (Markedly/Severely Ill).

## 5. Specific Phobias
- **Screening Tool**: Specific Phobia Questionnaire (SPQ) or Fear Survey Schedule (FSS) (General approaches due to specificity)
  - **Questions/Domains**: Assessment of marked fear concerning specific objects/situations (animals, environment, blood-injection, situational).
- **DSM-5 Criteria Summary**: Marked fear or anxiety about a specific object or situation. The object almost always provokes immediate fear, is actively avoided, and fear is out of proportion to actual danger. ≥6 months.
- **Validation Papers**: 
  - Arrindell WA, et al. "The Fear Survey Schedule (FSS-III): factorial invariance across sex..." *Behaviour Research and Therapy*, 2003;41(4):469-498.
- **User Language/Keywords**: "terrified of [X]", "freak out when I see [X]", "avoiding [X] at all costs".
- **Severity Scoring Interpretation**: Clinical cutoff based on functional impairment and avoidance severity.

## 6. Agoraphobia
- **Screening Tool**: Agoraphobic Cognitions Questionnaire (ACQ) / Mobility Inventory for Agoraphobia (MI)
  - **Questions/Domains**: Assessing fear of panic-related symptoms, avoidance of places alone or accompanied (e.g., public transport, open spaces, enclosed spaces).
- **DSM-5 Criteria Summary**: Marked fear/anxiety about ≥2 of 5 situations: using public transit, being in open spaces, being in enclosed spaces, standing in line/crowd, being outside of home alone. Fears situations because escape might be difficult.
- **Validation Papers**: 
  - Chambless DL, et al. "The Mobility Inventory for Agoraphobia." *Behaviour Research and Therapy*, 1985;23(1):35-44.
- **User Language/Keywords**: "can't leave the house", "trapped in crowds", "need a safe person", "scared of open spaces".
- **Severity Scoring Interpretation**: Scored on avoidance frequency (1=never avoid to 5=always avoid). High scores indicate severe impairment.

## 7. Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD)
- **Screening Tool**: PTSD Checklist for DSM-5 (PCL-5)
  - **Questions/Domains**: 20 items mapping to 4 DSM-5 symptom clusters: Intrusions, Avoidance, Negative alterations in cognitions/mood, Alterations in arousal/reactivity.
- **DSM-5 Criteria Summary**: Exposure to actual/threatened death, injury, or sexual violence. Presence of intrusion symptoms, avoidance of stimuli, negative alterations in cognitions/mood, and marked alterations in arousal/reactivity. Duration >1 month.
- **Validation Papers**: 
  - Blevins CA, et al. "The Posttraumatic Stress Disorder Checklist for DSM-5 (PCL-5): Development and Initial Psychometric Evaluation." *Journal of Traumatic Stress*, 2015;28(6):489-498. DOI: 10.1002/jts.22059
- **User Language/Keywords**: "flashbacks", "nightmares", "on high alert", "numb", "avoiding reminders", "startle easily".
- **Severity Scoring Interpretation**: 0-80 range. A cut-point score of 31-33 is generally considered indicative of probable PTSD.

## 8. Acute Stress Disorder
- **Screening Tool**: Acute Stress Disorder Scale (ASDS)
  - **Questions/Domains**: 19 items predicting PTSD, assessing dissociation, re-experiencing, avoidance, and arousal.
- **DSM-5 Criteria Summary**: Similar exposure criteria as PTSD. Presence of ≥9 of 14 symptoms from any of five categories: intrusion, negative mood, dissociation, avoidance, arousal. Duration is 3 days to 1 month after trauma exposure.
- **Validation Papers**: 
  - Bryant RA, et al. "The Acute Stress Disorder Scale: a self-report measure of acute stress disorder." *Psychological Assessment*, 2000;12(1):61-68.
- **User Language/Keywords**: "just happened", "in a daze", "unreal", "can't process it", "jittery since the accident".
- **Severity Scoring Interpretation**: A score of ≥9 for dissociation and ≥56 total often indicates clinical severity and PTSD risk.

## 9. Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD)
- **Screening Tool**: Yale-Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale (Y-BOCS)
  - **Questions/Domains**: 10 items assessing obsessions (time occupied, interference, distress, resistance, control) and compulsions (same 5 parameters).
- **DSM-5 Criteria Summary**: Presence of obsessions (recurrent intrusive thoughts) and/or compulsions (repetitive behaviors performed in response to obsession). Time-consuming (>1 hour/day) or cause significant distress.
- **Validation Papers**: 
  - Goodman WK, et al. "The Yale-Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale. I. Development, use, and reliability." *Archives of General Psychiatry*, 1989;46(11):1006-1011.
- **User Language/Keywords**: "can't stop thinking about", "have to do it or else", "rituals", "counting", "checking", "contamination".
- **Severity Scoring Interpretation**: 0-7 (Subclinical), 8-15 (Mild), 16-23 (Moderate), 24-31 (Severe), 32-40 (Extreme).

## 10. Body Dysmorphic Disorder (BDD)
- **Screening Tool**: Body Dysmorphic Disorder Questionnaire (BDDQ)
  - **Questions/Domains**: Assesses preoccupation with perceived defect, interference with social/occupational functioning, and repetitive behaviors (mirror checking).
- **DSM-5 Criteria Summary**: Preoccupation with ≥1 perceived defects/flaws in physical appearance not observable or appearing slight to others. Individual performs repetitive behaviors (e.g., mirror checking) or mental acts. Not better explained by eating disorder.
- **Validation Papers**: 
  - Phillips KA, et al. "The Body Dysmorphic Disorder Questionnaire." *Psychopharmacology Bulletin*, 1995.
- **User Language/Keywords**: "hideous", "obsessed with my nose/skin", "can't let people look at me", "mirror checking".
- **Severity Scoring Interpretation**: Categorical screening (Yes/No for probable BDD). If positive, followed by BDD-YBOCS for severity.

## 11. Hoarding Disorder
- **Screening Tool**: Hoarding Rating Scale (HRS)
  - **Questions/Domains**: 5 items assessing clutter, difficulty discarding, excessive acquisition, distress, and impairment.
- **DSM-5 Criteria Summary**: Persistent difficulty discarding/parting with possessions, regardless of actual value. Due to perceived need to save items and distress associated with discarding them. Results in accumulation of clutter compromising living areas.
- **Validation Papers**: 
  - Tolin DF, et al. "The Hoarding Rating Scale-Interview." *Psychiatry Research*, 2010;178(1):147-152.
- **User Language/Keywords**: "can't throw it away", "might need it later", "too much stuff", "running out of space".
- **Severity Scoring Interpretation**: Scores ≥14 generally indicate clinically significant hoarding.

## 12. Bipolar I and II Disorder
- **Screening Tool**: Mood Disorder Questionnaire (MDQ)
  - **Questions/Domains**: 13 yes/no questions about hypomanic/manic symptoms (e.g., reduced need for sleep, racing thoughts, risky behavior), plus questions on symptom co-occurrence and impairment.
- **DSM-5 Criteria Summary**: 
  - Bipolar I: At least one Manic Episode (elevated, expansive, or irritable mood, goal-directed activity/energy, lasting ≥1 week).
  - Bipolar II: At least one Hypomanic Episode (≥4 days) and at least one Major Depressive Episode. No full manic episode.
- **Validation Papers**: 
  - Hirschfeld RM, et al. "Development and validation of a screening instrument for bipolar spectrum disorder: the Mood Disorder Questionnaire." *American Journal of Psychiatry*, 2000;157(11):1873-1875.
- **User Language/Keywords**: "highs and lows", "no sleep but tons of energy", "racing mind", "impulsive decisions", "spending sprees".
- **Severity Scoring Interpretation**: Positive screen if: ≥7 of 13 symptoms answered "yes", PLUS symptoms co-occurred at the same time, PLUS moderate-to-severe impairment.

## 13. Cyclothymic Disorder
- **Screening Tool**: TEMPS-A (Temperament Evaluation of Memphis, Pisa, Paris and San Diego) for cyclothymic temperament.
- **DSM-5 Criteria Summary**: For ≥2 years (1 year in children/adolescents), numerous periods with hypomanic symptoms that do not meet criteria for a hypomanic episode and periods with depressive symptoms that do not meet criteria for major depressive episode. Symptoms present ≥50% of the time.
- **Validation Papers**: 
  - Akiskal HS, et al. "TEMPS-A: validation of a short version of a self-rated instrument designed to measure variations in temperament." *Journal of Affective Disorders*, 2005;85(1-2):45-52.
- **User Language/Keywords**: "mood swings", "unpredictable", "up and down all the time", "chronic instability".
- **Severity Scoring Interpretation**: Evaluated based on trait scales; high cyclothymic temperament score indicates risk/presence of disorder.

## 14. Persistent Depressive Disorder (Dysthymia)
- **Screening Tool**: PHQ-9 (modified duration assessment) or Cornell Dysthymia Rating Scale (CDRS).
- **DSM-5 Criteria Summary**: Depressed mood for most of the day, for more days than not, for at least 2 years. Presence of ≥2 of: poor appetite/overeating, insomnia/hypersomnia, low energy, low self-esteem, poor concentration, hopelessness.
- **Validation Papers**: 
  - Mason BJ, et al. "The Cornell Dysthymia Rating Scale: reliability, validity, and sensitivity to change." *Psychiatry Research*, 1993;47(1):73-85.
- **User Language/Keywords**: "always been this way", "mildly depressed forever", "Eeyore", "functioning but sad".
- **Severity Scoring Interpretation**: Standard scoring applies, with chronological modifiers emphasizing chronicity (>2 years) over acute severity.

## 15. Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder (PMDD)
- **Screening Tool**: Daily Record of Severity of Problems (DRSP)
  - **Questions/Domains**: Prospective daily rating of 11 DSM-5 symptoms across the menstrual cycle.
- **DSM-5 Criteria Summary**: In the majority of menstrual cycles, ≥5 symptoms present in the final week before onset of menses, start to improve within a few days after onset, and become minimal/absent postmenses. Must include ≥1 core mood symptom (lability, irritability, depressed mood, anxiety).
- **Validation Papers**: 
  - Endicott J, et al. "Is premenstrual dysphoric disorder a distinct clinical entity?" *Journal of Women's Health & Gender-Based Medicine*, 1999.
  - Endicott J, et al. "Daily Record of Severity of Problems (DRSP): reliability and validity." *Archives of Women's Mental Health*, 2006;9(1):41-49.
- **User Language/Keywords**: "Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde before period", "severe PMS", "uncontrollable rage before period", "monthly breakdown".
- **Severity Scoring Interpretation**: Confirmed if symptoms show a clear pattern of cyclic exacerbation (luteal phase) and remittance (follicular phase) over ≥2 symptomatic cycles, with significant distress/impairment.
