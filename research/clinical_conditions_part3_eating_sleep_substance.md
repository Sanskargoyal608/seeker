# Exhaustive Research Report: Eating, Sleep, Somatic, and Substance Use Disorders

This report details the screening tools, diagnostic criteria, validation, and user presentation for 14 psychological conditions across eating disorders, sleep-wake disorders, somatic symptom and related disorders, and substance-related and addictive disorders.

---

## 1. Anorexia Nervosa
**Gold-Standard Screening Tool:** Eating Attitudes Test-26 (EAT-26)

**Domains/Subscales:**
1. **Dieting:** Preoccupation with thinness, dieting behaviors, avoidance of high-calorie foods.
2. **Bulimia and Food Preoccupation:** Thoughts about food, binge-eating, or purging behaviors.
3. **Oral Control:** Self-control around food, perceived social pressure.

**Severity Scoring Interpretation:**
*   Items are scored on a 0-3 scale (Always=3, Usually=2, Often=1, Sometimes/Rarely/Never=0). Item 26 is reverse-scored.
*   **Clinical Cut-off:** Total score of ≥ 20 indicates a high level of concern regarding dieting, body weight, or problematic eating behaviors, warranting clinical referral.
*   *Note:* Extreme low weight or positive answers to behavioral questions (bingeing/purging) also trigger referral regardless of score.

**DSM-5 Diagnostic Criteria Summary:**
*   Restriction of energy intake relative to requirements, leading to a significantly low body weight.
*   Intense fear of gaining weight or becoming fat, or persistent behavior that interferes with weight gain.
*   Disturbance in the way one's body weight or shape is experienced.

**Validation Papers:**
*   Garner, D. M., et al. (1982). "The Eating Attitudes Test: psychometric features and clinical correlates." *Psychological Medicine*, 12(4), 871-878.
*   Mintz, L. B., & O'Halloran, M. S. (2000). "The Eating Attitudes Test: Validation with DSM-IV eating disorder criteria." *Journal of Personality Assessment*, 74(3), 489-503.

**Common User Language/Keywords:** "Fear of getting fat", "counting calories", "feeling huge", "skipping meals", "water fasting", "thigh gap".

## 2. Bulimia Nervosa
**Gold-Standard Screening Tool:** Bulimia Test-Revised (BULIT-R)

**Domains/Questions:**
Assesses symptoms related to bulimia including:
*   Binge eating episodes (frequency and severity)
*   Lack of control over eating
*   Compensatory behaviors (self-induced vomiting, laxative use, excessive exercise)
*   Disturbance in perception of body weight and shape

**Severity Scoring Interpretation:**
*   Consists of 36 items (28 scored) with 5 multiple-choice options. Total score ranges from 28 to 140.
*   **Clinical Cutoff:** A score of > 104 is highly indicative of bulimia nervosa.

**DSM-5 Diagnostic Criteria Summary:**
*   Recurrent episodes of binge eating (eating an objectively large amount of food with a sense of lack of control).
*   Recurrent inappropriate compensatory behaviors to prevent weight gain (vomiting, laxatives, fasting, overexercising).
*   Behaviors occur on average at least once a week for 3 months.
*   Self-evaluation is unduly influenced by body shape and weight.

**Validation Papers:**
*   Thelen, M. H., et al. (1991). "A revision of the Bulimia Test: The BULIT-R." *Psychological Assessment*, 3(1), 119-124.

**Common User Language/Keywords:** "Bingeing and purging", "throwing up after eating", "losing control with food", "laxatives to lose weight", "guilt after eating".

## 3. Binge Eating Disorder (BED)
**Gold-Standard Screening Tool:** Binge Eating Scale (BES)

**Domains/Subscales:**
1. **Behavioral:** Amount of food consumed, speed of eating, eating in secret, eating when not hungry.
2. **Cognitive & Affective:** Preoccupation with food, guilt, self-loathing, distress related to eating patterns, lack of control.

**Severity Scoring Interpretation:**
*   16 items scored 0-3 (or 0-2). Total range: 0-46.
*   **0–17:** No or minimal binge eating.
*   **18–26:** Moderate binge eating (sub-clinical or mild BED).
*   **27–46:** Severe binge eating (probable clinical BED).

**DSM-5 Diagnostic Criteria Summary:**
*   Recurrent episodes of binge eating (eating large amounts with loss of control).
*   Episodes are associated with eating rapidly, until uncomfortably full, when not hungry, alone out of embarrassment, and feeling disgusted/guilty afterward.
*   Marked distress regarding binge eating.
*   Occurs at least once a week for 3 months.
*   No regular use of compensatory behaviors.

**Validation Papers:**
*   Gormally, J., et al. (1982). "The assessment of binge eating severity among obese persons." *Addictive Behaviors*, 7(1), 47-55.

**Common User Language/Keywords:** "Eating until I'm sick", "can't stop eating", "sneaking food", "emotional eating", "stress eating", "feeling disgusted with myself".

## 4. Avoidant/Restrictive Food Intake Disorder (ARFID)
**Gold-Standard Screening Tool:** PARDI-AR-Q (Pica, ARFID, and Rumination Disorder Interview – ARFID Questionnaire)

**Domains/Subscales:**
1. **Sensory Sensitivity:** Avoidance due to taste, texture, smell, color.
2. **Lack of Interest:** Low appetite or disinterest in eating.
3. **Fear of Aversive Consequences:** Fear of choking, vomiting, or pain.

**Severity Scoring Interpretation:**
*   Uses Likert scales to measure symptom severity across domains and overall functional impairment. Higher scores indicate greater ARFID severity.

**DSM-5 Diagnostic Criteria Summary:**
*   An eating or feeding disturbance leading to persistent failure to meet nutritional/energy needs associated with: significant weight loss, significant nutritional deficiency, dependence on enteral feeding, or marked interference with psychosocial functioning.
*   Not explained by lack of food or cultural practice; does not occur exclusively during anorexia or bulimia.

**Validation Papers:**
*   Bryant-Waugh, R., et al. (2022). "Preliminary validation of the pica, ARFID and rumination disorder interview ARFID questionnaire (PARDI-AR-Q)." *Journal of Eating Disorders*, 10, Article 75.

**Common User Language/Keywords:** "Picky eater", "texture issues with food", "scared to choke", "food makes me gag", "no appetite", "safe foods only".

## 5. Insomnia Disorder
**Gold-Standard Screening Tool:** Insomnia Severity Index (ISI)

**Domains/Subscales:**
1. Severity of sleep onset difficulties.
2. Severity of sleep maintenance difficulties.
3. Severity of early morning awakening.
4. Sleep dissatisfaction.
5. Impact/Impairment (interference, noticeability, distress).

**Severity Scoring Interpretation:**
*   7 items, scored 0-4. Total score 0-28.
*   **0-7:** No clinically significant insomnia.
*   **8-14:** Sub-threshold insomnia.
*   **15-21:** Clinical insomnia (moderate severity).
*   **22-28:** Clinical insomnia (severe).

**DSM-5 Diagnostic Criteria Summary:**
*   Dissatisfaction with sleep quantity or quality (difficulty initiating, maintaining, or early-morning awakening).
*   Causes clinically significant distress or impairment.
*   Occurs at least 3 nights/week for at least 3 months.

**Validation Papers:**
*   Bastien, C. H., Vallières, A., & Morin, C. M. (2001). "Validation of the Insomnia Severity Index as an outcome measure for insomnia research." *Sleep Medicine*, 2(4), 297-307.

**Common User Language/Keywords:** "Can't fall asleep", "waking up at 3 AM", "tossing and turning", "tired all day", "mind racing at night".

## 6. Narcolepsy
**Gold-Standard Screening Tool:** Swiss Narcolepsy Scale (SNS)

**Domains/Subscales:**
1. **Excessive Daytime Sleepiness:** Nighttime sleep, morning restfulness, daytime napping.
2. **Cataplexy:** Muscle weakness triggered by emotions (laughter, anger).

**Severity Scoring Interpretation:**
*   Calculated via weighted equation: (6 × Q1) + (9 × Q2) – (5 × Q3) – (11 × Q4) – (13 × Q5) + 20.
*   **Score < 0:** Suggestive of narcolepsy with cataplexy (NT1).

**DSM-5 Diagnostic Criteria Summary:**
*   Recurrent periods of irrepressible need to sleep, lapsing into sleep, or napping, occurring at least 3 times/week over 3 months.
*   Presence of at least one: Cataplexy, Hypocretin deficiency, or REM sleep latency ≤ 15 minutes.

**Validation Papers:**
*   Sturzenegger, C., & Bassetti, C. L. (2004). "The clinical spectrum of narcolepsy with cataplexy: a reappraisal." *Journal of Sleep Research*, 13(4), 395-406.

**Common User Language/Keywords:** "Falling asleep constantly", "sleep attacks", "collapsing when laughing", "sleep paralysis", "hallucinations when falling asleep".

## 7. Sleep Apnea
**Gold-Standard Screening Tool:** STOP-Bang Questionnaire

**Domains/Subscales:**
*   **STOP (Symptoms):** Snoring, Tiredness, Observed apnea, Pressure (hypertension).
*   **Bang (Demographics):** BMI (>35), Age (>50), Neck circumference (>40cm), Gender (Male).

**Severity Scoring Interpretation:**
*   1 point for each "Yes". Total 0-8.
*   **0-2:** Low risk of OSA.
*   **3-4:** Intermediate risk.
*   **5-8:** High risk of OSA.

**DSM-5 Diagnostic Criteria Summary:**
*   Evidence by polysomnography of at least five obstructive apneas or hypopneas per hour of sleep, accompanied by nocturnal breathing disturbances or daytime sleepiness. OR
*   15 or more apneas/hypopneas per hour regardless of accompanying symptoms.

**Validation Papers:**
*   Chung, F., et al. (2008). "STOP questionnaire: a tool to screen patients for obstructive sleep apnea." *Anesthesiology*, 108(5), 812-821.

**Common User Language/Keywords:** "Snoring loudly", "partner says I stop breathing", "waking up gasping", "morning headaches".

## 8. Somatic Symptom Disorder
**Gold-Standard Screening Tool:** Patient Health Questionnaire-15 (PHQ-15)

**Domains/Subscales:**
Evaluates presence/severity of 15 somatic symptoms including pain, GI symptoms, fatigue, cardiopulmonary symptoms.

**Severity Scoring Interpretation:**
*   Each item scored 0-2 (0=Not bothered, 2=Bothered a lot). Range: 0-30.
*   **0-4:** Minimal.
*   **5-9:** Mild.
*   **10-14:** Moderate.
*   **15-30:** Severe. (Score ≥ 10 is the usual clinical cut-off).

**DSM-5 Diagnostic Criteria Summary:**
*   One or more somatic symptoms that are distressing or disrupt daily life.
*   Excessive thoughts, feelings, or behaviors related to the symptoms (disproportionate thoughts about seriousness, high anxiety, excessive time devoted).
*   Typically lasts > 6 months.

**Validation Papers:**
*   Kroenke, K., Spitzer, R. L., & Williams, J. B. (2002). "The PHQ-15: validity of a new measure for evaluating the severity of somatic symptoms." *Psychosomatic Medicine*, 64(2), 258-266.

**Common User Language/Keywords:** "Doctors can't find what's wrong", "mysterious pain", "chronic fatigue", "always have some physical problem".

## 9. Illness Anxiety Disorder (Hypochondria)
**Gold-Standard Screening Tool:** Whiteley Index (WI-6 or WI-7)

**Domains/Subscales:**
1. Bodily Preoccupation.
2. Disease Worry / Phobia.
3. Conviction of Disease (despite medical reassurance).

**Severity Scoring Interpretation:**
*   For the WI-6, a cutoff of **18** optimally balances sensitivity and specificity for severe health anxiety.

**DSM-5 Diagnostic Criteria Summary:**
*   Preoccupation with having or acquiring a serious illness.
*   Somatic symptoms are absent or mild.
*   High level of anxiety about health, easily alarmed.
*   Performs excessive health-related behaviors (checking) or exhibits maladaptive avoidance.

**Validation Papers:**
*   Pilowsky, I. (1967). "Dimensions of hypochondriasis." *The British Journal of Psychiatry*, 113(494), 89-93.

**Common User Language/Keywords:** "WebMD says I have cancer", "obsessed with my health", "checking my lymph nodes", "scared I'm dying".

## 10. Conversion Disorder (Functional Neurological Symptom Disorder)
**Gold-Standard Screening Tool:** No universally validated self-report screening tool exists. Diagnosis relies entirely on **clinical neurological examination** identifying positive signs (e.g., Hoover's sign).

**DSM-5 Diagnostic Criteria Summary:**
*   One or more symptoms of altered voluntary motor or sensory function.
*   Clinical findings provide evidence of incompatibility between the symptom and recognized neurological or medical conditions.
*   Causes clinically significant distress or impairment.

**Validation Papers:**
*   Stone, J., et al. (2005). "Systematic review of the diagnosis of functional neurologic symptoms." *Journal of Neurology, Neurosurgery & Psychiatry*.

**Common User Language/Keywords:** "Sudden paralysis", "psychogenic non-epileptic seizures", "can't walk but tests are normal", "functional tremor", "blindness from stress".

## 11. Substance Use Disorders (Alcohol)
**Gold-Standard Screening Tool:** Alcohol Use Disorders Identification Test (AUDIT)

**Domains/Subscales:**
1. Hazardous alcohol consumption.
2. Dependence symptoms (loss of control, morning drinking).
3. Harmful alcohol consumption (memory loss, injuries).

**Severity Scoring Interpretation:**
*   10 items, total score 0-40.
*   **0-7:** Low-risk.
*   **8-14:** Hazardous or harmful drinking.
*   **15+:** Likely moderate to severe Alcohol Use Disorder.

**DSM-5 Diagnostic Criteria Summary:**
*   Problematic pattern of alcohol use leading to clinically significant impairment, with at least 2 of 11 criteria within 12 months (e.g., taking more than intended, cravings, failure to fulfill obligations, tolerance, withdrawal).

**Validation Papers:**
*   Saunders, J. B., et al. (1993). "Development of the Alcohol Use Disorders Identification Test (AUDIT)..." *Addiction*, 88(6), 791-804.

**Common User Language/Keywords:** "Drinking too much", "blackouts", "needing a drink in the morning", "hiding bottles", "liver problems but can't stop".

## 12. Substance Use Disorders (Drugs)
**Gold-Standard Screening Tool:** Drug Abuse Screening Test (DAST-10)

**Domains/Subscales:**
Unidimensional tool covering consequences of drug use, loss of control, medical problems, and withdrawal.

**Severity Scoring Interpretation:**
*   10 Yes/No items. Range: 0-10.
*   **0:** No problems.
*   **1-2:** Low level.
*   **3-5:** Moderate level.
*   **6-8:** Substantial level.
*   **9-10:** Severe level. (Score of ≥ 3 prompts evaluation for DSM-5 SUD).

**DSM-5 Diagnostic Criteria Summary:**
*   Pattern of use leading to impairment/distress, 2+ criteria over 12 months. Criteria include impaired control, social impairment, risky use, and tolerance/withdrawal.

**Validation Papers:**
*   Skinner, H. A. (1982). "The drug abuse screening test." *Addictive Behaviors*, 7(4), 363-371.

**Common User Language/Keywords:** "Using pills", "getting high", "can't function without drugs", "spending all money on stuff", "dope sick", "withdrawal".

## 13. Gambling Disorder
**Gold-Standard Screening Tool:** Problem Gambling Severity Index (PGSI)

**Domains/Subscales:**
1. Gambling Behaviors (e.g., chasing losses).
2. Adverse Consequences (e.g., financial problems, health issues).

**Severity Scoring Interpretation:**
*   9 items scored 0-3. Range: 0-27.
*   **0:** Non-problem.
*   **1-2:** Low-risk.
*   **3-7:** Moderate-risk.
*   **8+:** Problem gambler.

**DSM-5 Diagnostic Criteria Summary:**
*   Persistent and recurrent problematic gambling leading to significant impairment, indicated by 4+ criteria in 12 months (e.g., needs to gamble with increasing amounts, restless when cutting down, chases losses, lies to conceal extent).

**Validation Papers:**
*   Ferris, J., & Wynne, H. (2001). "The Canadian Problem Gambling Index: Final report." *Canadian Centre on Substance Abuse*.

**Common User Language/Keywords:** "Chasing losses", "betting the house", "sports betting addiction", "hiding debt", "slots", "just one more bet".

---

## 14. Internet/Gaming Addiction
**Gold-Standard Screening Tool:** Internet Gaming Disorder Test (IGD-20)

**Domains/Subscales:**
1. Salience, 2. Mood Modification, 3. Tolerance, 4. Withdrawal Symptoms, 5. Conflict, 6. Relapse.

**Severity Scoring Interpretation:**
*   20 items, total score: 20-100.
*   **Clinical Cut-off:** Total score of ≥ 71 indicates potential Internet Gaming Disorder.

**DSM-5 Diagnostic Criteria Summary:**
*   (Condition for Further Study): Persistent use of the internet to engage in games, leading to clinically significant impairment/distress, 5+ criteria in 12 months.

**Validation Papers:**
*   Pontes, H. M., et al. (2014). "The conceptualisation and measurement of DSM-5 Internet Gaming Disorder: The development of the IGD-20 Test." *PLoS ONE*, 9(10), e110137.

**Common User Language/Keywords:** "Addicted to gaming", "playing 14 hours a day", "skipping school/work to play", "rage quitting", "ignoring real life for virtual".
