# Clinical Conditions Part 2: Neurodevelopmental, Psychotic, and Dissociative Disorders

This report details exhaustive research on specific neurodevelopmental, psychotic, and dissociative conditions, providing screening tools, DSM-5 criteria, research validations, user language, and scoring interpretation.

## I. Neurodevelopmental Disorders

### 1. ADHD (Adult)
*   **Gold-Standard Screening Tool:** Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale (ASRS v1.1). Consists of 18 items based on DSM-IV (applicable to DSM-5 with adjustments), split into Part A (6 items, most predictive) and Part B (12 items). Domains: Inattention, Hyperactivity/Impulsivity.
*   **DSM-5 Criteria Summary:** Five or more symptoms of inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity present for at least 6 months. Symptoms must be present before age 12, occur in two or more settings, and interfere with functioning.
*   **Validation Papers:**
    *   Kessler, R. C., et al. (2005). The World Health Organization Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale (ASRS): a short screening scale for use in the general population. *Psychological Medicine, 35*(2), 245-256. (DOI: 10.1017/s0033291704002892)
    *   Ustun, B., et al. (2017). The World Health Organization Adult Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder Self-Report Screening Scale for DSM-5. *JAMA Psychiatry, 74*(5), 520-526. (DOI: 10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2017.0298)
*   **Common User Language:** "Can't focus," "brain fog," "always fidgeting," "easily distracted," "procrastination," "rejection sensitive dysphoria," "hyperfixation."
*   **Severity Scoring:** For Part A, if 4 or more marks appear in the darkly shaded boxes (indicating high frequency of symptoms), the patient has symptoms highly consistent with ADHD in adults and warrants further investigation.

### 2. ADHD (Child/Adolescent)
*   **Gold-Standard Screening Tool:** Vanderbilt Assessment Scale and Conners Rating Scales. The Vanderbilt evaluates ADHD symptoms, ODD, Conduct Disorder, Anxiety, and Depression.
*   **DSM-5 Criteria Summary:** Six or more symptoms of inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity present for at least 6 months. Symptoms must have been present prior to age 12, occur in multiple settings, and cause functional impairment.
*   **Validation Papers:**
    *   Wolraich, M. L., et al. (2003). Psychometric properties of the Vanderbilt ADHD diagnostic parent rating scale in a clinical population. *Journal of Pediatric Psychology, 28*(8), 559-568.
    *   Conners, C. K., et al. (1998). The revised Conners' Parent Rating Scale (CPRS-R): factor structure, reliability, and criterion validity. *Journal of Abnormal Child Psychology, 26*(4), 257-268.
*   **Common User Language:** "Bouncing off walls," "won't listen," "can't sit still," "daydreaming in class," "constantly losing things," "acting out."
*   **Severity Scoring:** Vanderbilt: Symptom is counted if rated a 2 (Often) or 3 (Very Often). Needs 6 of 9 on either inattention or hyperactivity domains. Conners: T-scores > 65 indicate clinically significant problems.

### 3. Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD)
*   **Gold-Standard Screening Tool:** Autism-Spectrum Quotient (AQ-10) for brief screening; Ritvo Autism Asperger Diagnostic Scale-Revised (RAADS-R) for deeper self-report.
*   **DSM-5 Criteria Summary:** Persistent deficits in social communication and social interaction across multiple contexts. Restricted, repetitive patterns of behavior, interests, or activities. Symptoms must be present in the early developmental period and cause clinically significant impairment.
*   **Validation Papers:**
    *   Allison, C., et al. (2012). The Q-CHAT (Quantitative C-CHAT): A normally distributed quantitative measure of autistic traits at 18-24 months of age: preliminary report. *Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders, 42*(7), 1414-1425. (AQ-10 validation)
    *   Ritvo, R. A., et al. (2011). The Ritvo Autism Asperger Diagnostic Scale-Revised (RAADS-R): a scale to assist the diagnosis of autism spectrum disorder in adults: an international validation study. *Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders, 41*(8), 1076-1089.
*   **Common User Language:** "Sensory overload," "stimming," "special interests," "masking," "misses social cues," "needs strict routine."
*   **Severity Scoring:** AQ-10: Score > 6 warrants a full diagnostic assessment. RAADS-R: Score > 65 suggests a likely diagnosis of ASD, though high false-positive rates with comorbid conditions exist.

### 4. Intellectual Disability
*   **Screening Approaches:** No single self-report tool; relies on standardized cognitive testing (e.g., WAIS-IV, WISC-V) and adaptive functioning scales (e.g., Vineland Adaptive Behavior Scales).
*   **DSM-5 Criteria Summary:** Deficits in intellectual functions (reasoning, problem-solving, planning, abstract thinking) confirmed by clinical assessment and individualized standardized intelligence testing. Deficits in adaptive functioning that result in failure to meet developmental and sociocultural standards for personal independence.
*   **Validation Papers:**
    *   Sparrow, S. S., et al. (2016). *Vineland Adaptive Behavior Scales, Third Edition (Vineland-3)*. Pearson.
*   **Common User Language:** "Slow learner," "developmental delay," "struggles with daily tasks," "needs help managing money/hygiene."
*   **Severity Scoring:** Severity is based on adaptive functioning, not IQ scores alone, categorized into Mild, Moderate, Severe, and Profound.

### 5. Specific Learning Disorders
*   **Screening Approaches:** Woodcock-Johnson Tests of Achievement, Wechsler Individual Achievement Test (WIAT).
*   **DSM-5 Criteria Summary:** Difficulties learning and using academic skills, persisting for at least 6 months, despite interventions. Affected academic skills are substantially below those expected for chronological age. Specify if with impairment in reading (Dyslexia), written expression (Dysgraphia), or mathematics (Dyscalculia).
*   **Validation Papers:**
    *   Schrank, F. A., et al. (2014). *Woodcock-Johnson IV Tests of Achievement*. Riverside Assessments.
*   **Common User Language:** "Letters jumping around," "bad at math," "terrible spelling," "can't get thoughts on paper."
*   **Severity Scoring:** Standard scores on achievement tests substantially below expected standard scores for age/IQ (typically >1.5 standard deviations below the mean).

### 6. Tourette Syndrome / Tic Disorders
*   **Screening Approaches:** Yale Global Tic Severity Scale (YGTSS).
*   **DSM-5 Criteria Summary:** Both multiple motor and one or more vocal tics have been present at some time during the illness, though not necessarily concurrently. Tics may wax and wane but have persisted for more than 1 year. Onset before age 18.
*   **Validation Papers:**
    *   Leckman, J. F., et al. (1989). The Yale Global Tic Severity Scale: initial testing of a clinician-rated scale of tic severity. *Journal of the American Academy of Child & Adolescent Psychiatry, 28*(4), 566-573.
*   **Common User Language:** "Twitches," "blinking constantly," "making weird noises," "can't stop my body from doing it."
*   **Severity Scoring:** YGTSS yields specific scores for motor and vocal tics across domains like number, frequency, intensity, complexity, and interference (Scale 0-50), plus an impairment score (0-50).

## II. Schizophrenia Spectrum and Other Psychotic Disorders

### 7. Schizophrenia
*   **Gold-Standard Screening Tool:** PRIME Screen (Prodromal Questionnaire) for Clinical High Risk (CHR) of psychosis.
*   **DSM-5 Criteria Summary:** Two or more of the following for a significant portion of a 1-month period: delusions, hallucinations, disorganized speech, grossly disorganized or catatonic behavior, negative symptoms. Continuous signs of disturbance for at least 6 months.
*   **Validation Papers:**
    *   Miller, T. J., et al. (2004). The PRIME Screen and the PRIME Clinic: clinical assessment of the prodrome. *Schizophrenia Research, 71*(2-3), 263-267.
*   **Common User Language:** "Hearing voices," "people are watching me," "paranoia," "thought broadcasting," "losing touch with reality."
*   **Severity Scoring:** PRIME-Screen: Endorsement of 1 or more items with a "definitely agree" rating, or 3 or more with "somewhat agree", warrants follow-up with the SIPS (Structured Interview for Psychosis-Risk Syndromes).

### 8. Schizoaffective Disorder
*   **Screening Approaches:** SCID-5 (Structured Clinical Interview for DSM-5) to differentiate mood episodes vs. psychotic symptoms.
*   **DSM-5 Criteria Summary:** An uninterrupted period of illness during which there is a major mood episode (major depressive or manic) concurrent with Criterion A of schizophrenia. Delusions or hallucinations for 2 or more weeks in the absence of a major mood episode during the lifetime duration of the illness.
*   **Validation Papers:**
    *   Malaspina, D., et al. (2013). Schizoaffective disorder in the DSM-5. *Schizophrenia Research, 150*(1), 21-25.
*   **Common User Language:** "Manic and hallucinating," "severe depression with paranoia," "mixed up moods and thoughts."
*   **Severity Scoring:** Delineated clinically based on the frequency and severity of both the mood components and the psychotic components.

### 9. Delusional Disorder
*   **Screening Approaches:** Clinical interview (SCID-5); no specific standalone screener due to the nature of lack of insight.
*   **DSM-5 Criteria Summary:** The presence of one (or more) delusions with a duration of 1 month or longer. Criterion A for schizophrenia has never been met. Functioning is not markedly impaired, and behavior is not obviously bizarre or odd.
*   **Validation Papers:**
    *   Munro, A. (1999). *Delusional disorder: Paranoia and related illnesses*. Cambridge University Press.
*   **Common User Language:** "They are poisoning my food," "the neighbor is stalking me," "the government is tapping my phone" (without other psychotic symptoms).
*   **Severity Scoring:** Severity is rated by quantitative assessment of primary symptoms of psychosis (e.g., Clinician-Rated Dimensions of Psychosis Symptom Severity).

### 10. Brief Psychotic Disorder
*   **Screening Approaches:** Clinical assessment (SCID-5 CV).
*   **DSM-5 Criteria Summary:** Presence of one or more: delusions, hallucinations, disorganized speech, grossly disorganized or catatonic behavior. Duration of an episode of the disturbance is at least 1 day but less than 1 month, with eventual full return to the premorbid level of functioning.
*   **Validation Papers:**
    *   Susser, E., et al. (1995). The epidemiology of brief psychotic disorder. *Schizophrenia Bulletin, 21*(1), 69-78.
*   **Common User Language:** "Brief psychotic break," "snapped," "nervous breakdown with hallucinations."
*   **Severity Scoring:** Rated clinically based on the acuteness and impairment of the brief episode.

## III. Dissociative Disorders

### 11. Dissociative Identity Disorder (DID)
*   **Gold-Standard Screening Tool:** Dissociative Experiences Scale-II (DES-II).
*   **DSM-5 Criteria Summary:** Disruption of identity characterized by two or more distinct personality states. Recurrent gaps in the recall of everyday events, important personal information, and/or traumatic events.
*   **Validation Papers:**
    *   Carlson, E. B., & Putnam, F. W. (1993). An update on the Dissociative Experiences Scale. *Dissociation, 6*(1), 16-27.
    *   Steinberg, M., et al. (1991). The Structured Clinical Interview for DSM-III-R Dissociative Disorders (SCID-D). *American Journal of Psychiatry, 148*, 377-377.
*   **Common User Language:** "Multiple personalities," "losing time," "alters," "feeling like someone else took over my body," "finding things I don't remember buying."
*   **Severity Scoring:** DES-II: A score above 30 indicates high levels of dissociation and suggests the need for a formal diagnostic interview (like the SCID-D) for DID.

### 12. Depersonalization/Derealization Disorder
*   **Screening Approaches:** Cambridge Depersonalization Scale (CDS); DES-II (specifically the depersonalization subscale).
*   **DSM-5 Criteria Summary:** The presence of persistent or recurrent experiences of depersonalization, derealization, or both. During the depersonalization or derealization experiences, reality testing remains intact.
*   **Validation Papers:**
    *   Sierra, M., & Berrios, G. E. (2000). The Cambridge Depersonalization Scale: a new instrument for the measurement of depersonalization. *Psychiatry Research, 93*(2), 153-164.
*   **Common User Language:** "Feeling like I'm in a dream," "out of body experience," "the world looks fake," "robot mode," "disconnected from reality."
*   **Severity Scoring:** CDS: Cut-off score of >70 distinguishes depersonalization disorder from other psychiatric conditions with high sensitivity and specificity.

### 13. Dissociative Amnesia
*   **Screening Approaches:** DES-II (amnesia subscale).
*   **DSM-5 Criteria Summary:** An inability to recall important autobiographical information, usually of a traumatic or stressful nature, that is inconsistent with ordinary forgetting.
*   **Validation Papers:**
    *   Spiegel, D., et al. (2011). Dissociative disorders in DSM-5. *Depression and Anxiety, 28*(12), 824-852.
*   **Common User Language:** "Blocked out a whole year," "blackout but wasn't drunk," "no memory of my childhood."
*   **Severity Scoring:** Qualitative clinical assessment of the extent of the amnesia (localized, selective, or generalized) and impact on functioning.
